# AIGC XPRIZE AI Operations Narrative — Draft V1

Status: `DRAFT — ARCHITECTURE EVIDENCED / LIVE GEMINI & CLOUD RECEIPTS PENDING`

AIGC — the Artificial Intelligence Gladiator Coliseum — is designed as a governed operating system for turning economic opportunities into auditable work. The competition-specific Project, **AIGC — Governed Gemini Opportunity Assessment**, focuses that broader architecture into a bounded business workflow: an opportunity is ingested, normalized, assessed with AI assistance, checked against policy, presented for accountable human decisions, and preserved with evidence that separates what the system planned from what was actually authorized and executed.

The central operating principle is that AI output is not authority. AIGC separates five concepts that are often collapsed in autonomous-agent systems: reasoning, verification, authority, execution, and evidence. An AI agent may analyze an opportunity, propose a next action, or generate an artifact, but it cannot convert its own recommendation into permission to perform a consequential external action. Material actions require an independent governance decision and, where applicable, a bounded execution permit. The permit is intended to bind capability, target resource, exact revision, request identity, invocation count, expiry, and cost ceiling. Execution then produces a receipt. This structure is meant to make the system useful for business operations without treating model confidence as permission.

The current Nexus V1 repository implementation includes the governed orchestration and provider-runtime foundations, including a Google Vertex Gemini adapter path, durable runtime-control design, provider-neutral execution contracts, evidence handling, emergency-stop and budget concepts, repository intelligence, and a bounded provider-canary procedure. That implementation has exact-head repository CI evidence on the Nexus release candidate. However, the project is deliberately not claiming that repository implementation equals a deployed business. The required empirical Google Cloud deployment and permit-bound live Gemini receipt remain submission gates at the time of this draft.

Within the intended live workflow, Gemini's role is analytical and generative rather than constitutional. A bounded request can ask Gemini to assess an opportunity, extract relevant structure, compare criteria, or produce a candidate analysis. AIGC then places the result inside a governed state transition rather than accepting the provider response as self-validating truth. The runtime records request/result identity, applies deterministic policy and budget limits, and routes consequential outcomes through verification and human authority. Provider credentials are kept outside prompts and normal evidence artifacts. A live competition proof is planned as a single task with one invocation, no automatic retry after an uncertain provider outcome, an explicit maximum cost, and a retained usage/latency/cost receipt.

Human responsibility remains explicit. The accountable operator determines commercial policy, cloud spending, deployment authority, material external actions, and final submission claims. A human approval is not treated as proof that an action occurred; execution still requires a receipt. Conversely, evidence that code or infrastructure exists does not grant authority to use it. This distinction is also applied to Revenue-Ω, AIGC's commercial architecture. The current software can calculate employer-funded success/placement economics, recover compute/API costs, create deterministic invoice candidates, simulate settlement without real money, and construct evidence-bound financial reports. Real employment-agency activity, invoice sending, payment collection, tax treatment, and owner distributions remain separately gated by licensing, commercial, payment-provider, accounting, and authorization requirements.

AIGC's operational design is intentionally evidence-heavy because the Project is trying to solve two problems at once: increasing the amount of useful economic work AI can help coordinate, and making that assistance auditable enough for people and businesses to trust. The desired production loop is not simply prompt → model → action. It is closer to opportunity → structured work order → context → candidate analysis/artifact → verification → governance decision → bounded permit → execution → receipt → updated state. Failures, missing authority, stale CI, moved revisions, indeterminate provider outcomes, and missing receipts are designed to stop progression instead of being rounded up into success.

For the XPRIZE demonstration, the evidence target is intentionally narrow. AIGC must first prove the exact repository candidate, then verify the Canadian Google Cloud project and billing relationship, deploy an immutable no-public-traffic staging candidate in Montréal, reach a real readiness state, execute one separately authorized Gemini call, retain the resulting technical and cost evidence, and then exercise the workflow with a real third-party pilot. Only after those receipts exist should the public Devpost copy use language such as “deployed,” “live Gemini,” “real user,” or “customer.” Until then, the strongest accurate claim is that AIGC has implemented and validated substantial governed architecture while the external runtime and business proof remain explicit completion gates.

The long-term business objective is to make opportunity assessment and work procurement more executable while preserving human authority over material consequences. If the pilot and commercial evidence support that hypothesis, AIGC can evolve toward a platform where AI increases the number and quality of opportunities a person or business can assess, while deterministic controls, evidence receipts, and employer/client-funded economics keep the system accountable and sustainable.
